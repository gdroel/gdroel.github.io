Gabe Roeloffs

Technology entrepreneur and angel investor

Gabe Roeloffs is the co-founder and CEO of ZeroSettle, a Y Combinator Winter 2026 company. He grew up in Arroyo Grande, California, released his first iPhone app in high school, and spent four years working on Apple operating systems before starting ZeroSettle.[1] [2] [3]

Early life

Roeloffs grew up in Arroyo Grande, a small farming town on California’s Central Coast. He credits his upbringing there with teaching him the value of hard work and resilience. When an iPad sparked his curiosity about how apps were made, he began learning iOS development at age 11.[3] [4]

In his early portfolio, he listed surfing and tennis among his favorite things to do outside of software.[5]

Early adolescence and high school

The Bruh App

Roeloffs built his first app, Bruh, in high school. The idea was simple. Tap a friend’s name and their iPhone would say “Bruh!” It became by far the most successful of his early projects, reaching thousands of users. He recalls the experience of getting his first app into people’s hands as intoxicating. When he introduced a feature called Bae Mode, he taught himself how to implement in-app purchases.[3]

In January 2015 he published a walkthrough of how he built Bruh, covering its interface, push notifications, and the Parse backend that handled friendships and blocking. A preserved listing records the app in the App Store’s Social Networking category.[6] [13]

College

From 2017 to 2021, Roeloffs studied computer science at California Polytechnic State University, San Luis Obispo, where he made the President’s List and Dean’s List. He recalls often skipping class to work on personal projects. At Cal Poly he met Ryan Elliott, a computer science classmate who became a friend and later his ZeroSettle co-founder.[2] [3] [7]

Apple

Apple was Roeloffs’s first job after college. From 2021 to 2025 he worked as a software engineer on Apple operating systems. He says the achievement he’s proudest of is that code he wrote at Apple still runs around the clock on two billion Apple devices.[2] [3] [8]

Transit and Apple Pay

Roeloffs worked on Apple Pay and transit features, including the iOS 16 Maps integration that let riders add transit cards to Wallet and reload them without leaving Maps. He also worked on next-stop suggestions for multistop routing and diagnosed memory growth in Maps and its destination prediction service.[2] [3]

ZeroSettle and Y Combinator

Roeloffs started ZeroSettle with his friend and Cal Poly computer science classmate Ryan Elliott. Both had worked as software engineers at Apple. Roeloffs became CEO and Elliott became CTO. The company joined Y Combinator’s Winter 2026 batch and raised a seed round from Y Combinator and other investors whose names have yet to be announced.[1] [3] [7]

ZeroSettle launched with a direct billing SDK that let mobile apps offer web checkout for in-app purchases. The launch described tools for subscription management, switching existing subscribers to direct billing, and payment processing.[9]

The company later introduced dedicated iMessage lines for businesses and AI agents, starting at $49 per month per line. Teams can send and receive messages through an API or connect an agent via Model Context Protocol (MCP). ZeroSettle lists customer support, appointment reminders, and sign-in by text among its use cases.[10]

Angel investing

Alongside running ZeroSettle, Roeloffs is an active angel investor. His public profile is concentrated in technology and startup circles, where he is known for his early apps, his engineering work at Apple, and co-founding ZeroSettle.[3] [11]
